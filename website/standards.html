Standards & compliance alignment

What ClaimCheck does, described in the terms existing standards already use — and an honest list of what it does not do.

The technique, in standards terms

ClaimCheck performs pseudonymization: direct identifiers are replaced with stable, reversible placeholders ([FEIN_1], [SSN_2]) and the mapping is kept in a local vault. That is:

  • ISO/IEC 20889:2018 — a reversible pseudonymization technique (mapping-based tokenization). This standard defines the vocabulary; ClaimCheck is a local, deterministic instance of it.
  • NIST SP 800-188 (De-Identifying Government Datasets) — describes the same technique family and the re-identification risk that stays with whoever holds the mapping. ClaimCheck's design keeps the mapping on the user's machine, so the re-identification risk stays with the data owner — not a vendor.
  • GDPR Art. 4(5) and Art. 32 — pseudonymization is explicitly named as a safeguard measure. Pseudonymized data is still personal data; ClaimCheck reduces exposure, it does not make data anonymous.

Scrubbing happens before text leaves the machine, and restoration happens after the model's response arrives — including mid-stream. No third party ever sees the mapping.

NIST alignment

SP 800-122 (Protecting the Confidentiality of PII)

ClaimCheck's recognizers map to SP 800-122's identifier categories: direct identifiers (SSN, FEIN, email, phone, passport, license, account numbers) are matched exactly; indirect identifiers (dates, addresses, organization names) are matched where they appear in labeled or structured form. SP 800-122 recommends reducing PII exposure at the point of use — that is precisely the proxy's job: strip before transmit, restore after response.

SP 800-57 (Key Management)

The vault mapping is the sensitive material. It never leaves the machine and lives under the user's profile (~/.sanitize-mcp/vaults/ or %USERPROFILE%\.sanitize-mcp\vaults\). Its protection is the user's existing disk-encryption and access-controls posture — ClaimCheck adds no network exposure to it.

SP 800-188 (De-Identification)

800-188's core warning is that de-identified data can be re-linked when mappings leak. ClaimCheck's answer is architectural: the mapping never crosses the wire, so the leak surface is the local disk only.

Honest limits

  • Pseudonymized ≠ anonymous. Anyone with the vault can restore everything. Treat the vault as the crown jewels: same sensitivity as the original data, on your disk.
  • Alignment ≠ certification. No certification path exists for PII-detection tools. ClaimCheck uses the standards' vocabulary and identifier lists; it is not "HIPAA certified" (that phrase doesn't exist for software) or PCI-certified.
  • Detection is probabilistic. Regex + an optional local NER model catch common shapes. Novel formats, unlabeled free-text identifiers, and heavily encoded values can slip through. Use the smoke-test prompt to validate against your data shapes.
  • Dates are partial. Labeled dates (DOB, expiry) are caught; a bare date in prose generally is not.
  • Vehicle, device and biometric identifiers are out of scope for text scrubbing today.
  • Complementary, not a DLP replacement. Email filters and DLP gateways enforce policy on the wire; ClaimCheck removes the PII before the wire exists. Use both.
